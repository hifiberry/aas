# AES67 input and output: design

## Intent

HiFiBerry OS receives AES67 today through PipeWire:

- The `hifiberry-aes67` package loads PipeWire's `module-rtp-sap`.
- It lists the streams announced over SAP.
- It links the one the user selects to the default sink.

Under `aas` none of that works, so a device that uses AES67 stays on PipeWire
(`doc/design.md`, "Non-goals for the first version").

The owner decided (2026-11-23):

- **AES67 moves into `aas` and leaves PipeWire.** The `hifiberry-aes67`
  package and its PipeWire drop-in are retired; AES67 exists only under `aas`.
- **Both directions.** `aas` receives an AES67 stream as a source in its mix,
  and sends what its master plays as an AES67 stream.
- **Time comes from PTP, behind a clock layer that supports more than one
  source.** The default is a passive listener inside `aas`. It reads the
  grandmaster's PTP messages, sends none, and runs beside `nqptp`, so
  Shairport Sync's AirPlay 2 reception keeps working. `ptp4l` (linuxptp) is a
  second source, for networks where `aas` itself must be the grandmaster, and
  comes in a second stage.

## Goals

- Everything `hifiberry-aes67` does today, under `aas`:
  - discover streams announced over SAP;
  - select one;
  - play it with a configurable latency whose default depends on the board;
  - report it to the web UI and to ACR.
- The selected stream as its own source in the mix, with its own volume and
  mute, and its own routes in the matrix.
- An AES67 sender: what the master plays, as a multicast stream announced
  over SAP, that a Dante device in AES67 mode can subscribe to in Dante
  Controller.
- Time from PTP through a clock layer:
  - `listen` (default, stage 1);
  - `ptp4l` (stage 2);
  - `off`.
- Without PTP, input still plays. It follows the sender's rate by the depth
  of its buffer, as PipeWire does today.

## Stages

1. **Stage 1:**
   - the clock layer with `listen` and `off`;
   - SAP and SDP;
   - input and output;
   - config, API, ACR;
   - `aas`'s own packaging changes.

   Confirmed on the owner's Dante gear (below).
2. **Stage 2:** the `ptp4l` source, both following and as grandmaster, and its
   packaging. A device then chooses between AirPlay 2 reception (`nqptp`) and
   `ptp4l`.
3. **Outside this repository, after stage 1** (see the last section):
   - retiring `hifiberry-aes67`;
   - the web UI page;
   - the player descriptor.

## Non-goals

- **A PTP implementation.** The listener only receives: Announce, Sync and
  Follow_Up. It sends no Delay_Req, takes no part in choosing the master, and
  is never a master. An in-process PTP (for example the `statime` crate) is
  possible later as another source, and is not built.
- **Path delay.** Like `nqptp`, the listener does not measure it. Its time is
  late by the one-way network delay, typically tens of µs. That is far inside
  AES67's link offsets of 1 ms or more, so playback is unaffected. Phase
  alignment with other Dante receivers is not.
- **More than one received stream at a time.** The old package plays one;
  so does this.
- **Other formats and protocols:**
  - rates other than 48 kHz on the wire;
  - one-step PTP masters;
  - ST 2110-30 profiles;
  - RAVENNA's RTSP discovery;
  - unicast streams;
  - Dante's own protocol.

  Dante in AES67 mode uses two-step PTPv2, 48 kHz multicast and SAP, and
  that is the target.
- **AES67 over Wi-Fi.** Wired Ethernet only, as today.
- **`nqptp`.** It is not used for AES67 (see Constraints for why), and is
  not changed.

## Anti-goals

- **Nothing on the network may disturb the audio.** Receiving, sending, SAP
  and the PTP listener run on their own threads. The audio thread only reads
  from and writes to rings, as for players and taps today. None of the
  following may cost more than silence on that source or a paused stream,
  never a gap on the master:
  - a lost packet;
  - a stream that stops;
  - a grandmaster that changes;
  - PTP that disappears.
- **Nothing `aas` sends may disturb the AES67 network.**
  - With `listen`, `aas` sends no PTP message at all.
  - It never sends audio stamped with a clock it does not follow: without PTP
    time, the output sends nothing.
- **AirPlay 2 reception keeps working.** With `listen`, `nqptp` and Shairport
  Sync are untouched.

## Constraints

- **Boards** (checked 2026-11-23):
  - The CM5 on kernel 6.18 reports hardware PTP timestamps and two PTP
    hardware clocks. The old package's README says PTP cannot work there; that
    was written against an older kernel.
  - The Pi 4 reports software transmit and receive timestamps.
- **`nqptp`** ships inside `hifiberry-shairport` (1.2.9, shared-memory
  interface 11) and holds UDP 319 and 320 alone. It cannot follow Dante. In
  its source (`nqptp.c`, 1.2.9) and in a test against the owner's Dante
  device on 2026-11-23:
  - It drops every PTP message whose source port differs from its
    destination port. Dante sends from ephemeral ports (seen: 56521→320,
    58122→319). Apple devices send 319→319.
  - It follows only the first address a client names.
  - It joins no multicast group.
  - It sends a clock it meets two unicast Announce messages, one claiming a
    better `priority1`. Against Dante this never fired, because of the port
    check.
- **The passive listener was tried on 2026-11-23** on the Pi 4, beside a
  running `nqptp`, against the owner's Dante device in AES67 mode, as a
  Python script with an `AF_PACKET` socket, `SO_TIMESTAMPNS`, and a join of
  224.0.1.129:
  - It found the grandmaster by itself: `00-11-22-ff-fe-33-44-55`, domain 0,
    `priority1` 247, two-step, multicast, Announce once a second.
  - Over 60 s, PTP against the Pi's `CLOCK_REALTIME` drifted a steady
    −16.3 ppm, within ±0.2 ppm after the first 10 s.
  - `nqptp` stayed active with its sockets, and the Pi sent no PTP packet.
  - Dante's PTP time was about 57 years behind the Pi's clock. Dante runs an
    arbitrary timescale, so no arithmetic may assume PTP time is a date.
  - Only 240 of 754 Syncs had a matching Follow_Up by sequence number. Four
    pairs a second is enough, but the builder finds out why (other senders
    on 319, or a matching bug in the script) before relying on it.
- **`linuxptp`** 4.2 is in Debian trixie (stage 2).
  - `ptp4l -S` (software timestamping) needs `SOF_TIMESTAMPING_TX_SOFTWARE`.
    The Pi 4 has it; the CM5 uses its hardware clock.
  - Other processes query it with management messages over its Unix socket:
    `TIME_STATUS_NP` and `PARENT_DATA_SET`, the way `pmc` does.
- **AES67 as Dante sends and takes it:**
  - L24 (or L16) big-endian, 48 kHz, packet time 1 ms (48 frames);
  - up to 8 channels per flow;
  - multicast, by default in 239.69.0.0/16;
  - announced over SAP on 239.255.255.255:9875, with an SDP carrying:
    - `a=rtpmap:<pt> L24/48000/<ch>`
    - `a=ptime:1`
    - `a=ts-refclk:ptp=IEEE1588-2008:<gm-clock-id>:<domain>`
    - `a=mediaclk:direct=<offset>`
  - The RTP timestamp of a sample is its media-clock time,
    `(PTP time × 48000 + offset) mod 2³²`.
  - A Dante receiver takes only a stream whose `ts-refclk` names the
    grandmaster it follows.
- **Switches** need IGMP snooping with a querier, or multicast floods every
  port. That is the network's job, and the README says so.
- **`aas`:**
  - **Rules:**
    - The audio thread must not allocate, free or lock (`engine.rs:1-5`).
    - `aas.service` runs with `DynamicUser=yes` and group `audio`, and has
      no capabilities today.
  - **What exists:**
    - The master's audio leaves through monitor taps
      (`Taps::deliver_monitor`, `engine.rs:892`): S32 stereo at the master
      rate, in an `aas_client::Ring`, as the AirPlay output reads it.
    - The matrix has four inputs (`players.*`, `analog.*`, `matrix.rs:11`).
    - `airplay/rtp.rs` builds RTP headers.
    - `adaptive.rs` with `drift.rs` is the resampler and controller that the
      further outputs and AirPlay use against a foreign clock.
  - **What is missing:**
    - No source today has drift control. Players are paced by the master,
      and the analog input shares its clock.
    - There is no multicast, SDP parser, SAP, PTP or RTP-receive code.
- **Latency defaults**, measured for the old package (`board.py`):
  - 3 ms on a CM5 or Pi 5;
  - 20 ms on a Pi 4, CM4 or any other board (3 ms gave 1320 xruns on a
    Pi 4).

## How it is confirmed

On the owner's Dante gear, with the test device on the same network as the
Dante device:

- **Clock:** `GET /aes67` shows the Dante grandmaster, locked, with a drift
  that matches the listener test (a few tens of ppm, steady). tcpdump shows
  no PTP packet sent by the device.
- **Input:**
  - The owner creates an AES67 multicast flow on the Dante device.
  - `aas` lists it, it is selected, and it plays for an hour with no xrun on
    the master.
  - It plays on after the Dante device restarts.
- **Output:**
  - Dante Controller lists the `aas` flow.
  - A Dante receiver subscribes and plays it for an hour without a dropout.
  - Dante Controller shows no clock or latency error for it.
- **Coexistence:** an iPhone plays to Shairport Sync on the same device
  while AES67 input or output runs.

## Approaches

1. **`nqptp` as the clock source.**
   - Pro: it already runs and costs no port.
   - Dropped after the test: it cannot follow Dante (source-port check,
     first address only, no multicast join). Making it work means patching
     a GPL-2.0 program inside `hifiberry-shairport`, which AirPlay 2 depends
     on.
2. **A passive listener inside `aas`, `ptp4l` as a second source**
   *(picked)*.
   - Pro: reads the grandmaster's messages through a packet socket, so it
     needs no port and runs beside `nqptp`.
   - Pro: sends nothing.
   - Pro: finds the grandmaster without an address.
   - Con: `aas` gains `CAP_NET_RAW`, and a few hundred lines of
     receive-only parsing.
3. **`ptp4l` only.**
   - Pro: a real follower, with path delay; grandmaster mode on every board.
   - Con: it takes ports 319/320, so AirPlay 2 reception stops on every
     device with AES67. Kept as the stage 2 source, chosen per device.
4. **No PTP, as PipeWire does today.**
   - Con: input only. No Dante receiver takes a stream whose timestamps
     follow no grandmaster.

## Design

### The PTP clock layer

One object, shared by the AES67 threads, answers:

```
now() -> Option<PtpNow { local_ns, ptp_ns, grandmaster: ClockId, domain }>
```

`local_ns` is the local clock the source measures against: `CLOCK_REALTIME`
for `listen`, as the kernel timestamps packets, converted to
`CLOCK_MONOTONIC_RAW` for callers if the builder prefers. `None` means no
usable PTP time:

- no grandmaster yet;
- none heard for 5 s;
- `ptp = "off"`.

Every caller handles `None`. A change of grandmaster is reported, so the
input can re-anchor and the output can announce its SDP again with the new
`ts-refclk`.

**`listen`**, one thread, `aas-ptp`, at normal priority:

- **Socket:**
  - Joins 224.0.1.129 on the configured interface.
  - Opens an `AF_PACKET` socket on that interface with a BPF filter: IPv4,
    UDP, destination 224.0.1.129, port 319 or 320.
  - Sets `SO_TIMESTAMPNS`.
- **Grandmaster:**
  - Takes Announce, Sync and Follow_Up on the configured domain (default 0).
  - With more than one master announcing, it follows the best by the
    standard dataset comparison of the Announce fields (`priority1`, clock
    class, accuracy, variance, `priority2`, identity). That is the master the
    Dante devices follow too.
- **Offset:**
  - Pairs Sync and Follow_Up by source and sequence number.
  - The offset is `preciseOriginTimestamp + correction fields − receive
    time`.
  - Smoothed over a window, with outliers dropped, as `nqptp` does. The
    builder picks the filter.
  - The rate between local and PTP time comes from the slope over that
    window.
- **Capability:** `aas.service` gains `AmbientCapabilities=CAP_NET_RAW`
  (and `CapabilityBoundingSet` limited to it). The thread opens the socket at
  start; nothing else in `aas` uses the capability.

**`ptp4l`** (stage 2): run by its own unit, set up by packaging, not by `aas`.

- `aas` queries `TIME_STATUS_NP` and `PARENT_DATA_SET` over ptp4l's
  read-only Unix socket about once a second, for the grandmaster's identity
  and whether it is locked.
- Time comes from the clock ptp4l steers:
  - on the CM5, its hardware clock, read with `clock_gettime` on its dynamic
    clock ID;
  - with `-S`, `CLOCK_REALTIME`.
- As grandmaster, `aas` reads the same clock.
- Choosing `ptp4l` means masking `nqptp`, so AirPlay 2 reception stops; the
  web UI must say so before the switch.

**`off`:** always `None`.

### SAP and SDP

One thread, `aas-sap`:

- **Joins** 239.255.255.255:9875 on the configured interface.
- **Parses** SAP packets (RFC 2974) and their SDP, only what AES67 needs:
  - origin;
  - connection address and TTL;
  - media port and payload type;
  - `rtpmap` (encoding and channels);
  - `ptime`;
  - `ts-refclk`;
  - `mediaclk`;
  - session name.
- **Keeps the list of streams.** A stream is removed on a deletion message,
  or after ten announcement intervals without one, at most an hour.
- **Announces the `aas` output** every 30 s while it sends, and sends a
  deletion when it stops.

A stream's name is its SDP session name (`s=`), as the old API shows it
(`"AU-U22-000001 : 1"`).

### Input

One thread, `aas-aes67-in`, at real-time priority 65 (below the audio
thread, like a further output's writer).

- **Receive:**
  - Joins the selected stream's group and port.
  - Reads RTP with `SO_TIMESTAMPNS`.
  - Takes L16 and L24 at 48 kHz, 1 to 8 channels, any packet time up to 4 ms.
  - Any other format is listed as not selectable.
- **Channels:** two channels are taken from the flow, set by the config key
  `channels` (default `[1, 2]`). A mono flow feeds both.
- **Buffer:** a jitter buffer keyed by RTP timestamp.
  - A lost packet becomes silence in its place, so the timing never slips.
  - A packet that arrives after its play time is dropped and counted.
- **Rate to the master:** an `Adaptive` resampler from 48 kHz to the
  master's rate, steered by a `Drift` controller, as a further output is.
  - **PTP mode:** the stream's `ts-refclk` names the grandmaster the clock
    layer follows.
    - The controller holds at `latency_ms` the delay between a sample's
      media-clock time and the local time it reaches the master.
    - The media-clock time comes from its RTP timestamp, the PTP time and
      the SDP `mediaclk` offset.
    - Where it reaches the master comes from the ring's fill and the
      engine's output delay.
  - **Buffer mode:** no PTP time, or the stream names another grandmaster
    or domain. The controller holds the jitter buffer's depth at
    `latency_ms`, as PipeWire does today. Such a stream stays selectable
    and plays. *(call)*
  - The mode is chosen when the stream starts.
  - When PTP time is lost or the grandmaster changes while it plays, the
    input goes silent, drops its buffer, and starts again in whichever mode
    now applies. A short gap on this source; no step in pitch. *(call)*
- **Into the engine:**
  - The resampled S32 stereo goes into an `aas_client::Ring`.
  - The engine reads it as a source on a new bus, `aes67`, paced by the
    master as a player is, woken by the ring's eventfd.
  - The engine sees no difference between this and a player, except the
    bus.
- **The `aes67` bus:**
  - It enters the matrix as `aes67.left` and `aes67.right`, so `INPUTS`
    grows from 4 to 6.
  - A matrix that names no `aes67` route gets the `players` default for it:
    left to channel 1, right to channel 2, at 0 dB. Otherwise selecting a
    stream on a device whose config predates this plays nothing. *(call)*
  - It has its own volume and mute, like the analog input.
  - It counts as a signal for `idle`.
- **When the stream stops** (no packet for 1 s):
  - The source plays silence.
  - The API reports `receiving: false`.
  - The thread keeps its membership and waits. When packets return, it
    starts again.

### Output

One thread, `aas-aes67-out`, at real-time priority 65.

- **Tap:** a monitor tap with `source = 0`, as the AirPlay output's: what the
  master card gets, S32 stereo at the master rate.
- **Rate:** an `Adaptive` from the master's rate to 48 kHz. Its `Drift`
  controller holds the tap-to-send delay steady against PTP time, so the
  card's crystal is absorbed against the grandmaster.
- **Packets:**
  - 48 frames of L24 stereo for every 1 ms of PTP time.
  - Sent to a multicast group and port from the config. The default group
    is in 239.69.0.0/16, derived from the interface's MAC so two devices do
    not collide; the default port is 5004.
  - TTL from the config, default 16.
  - The RTP timestamp is the media-clock time of the packet's first frame,
    `(ptp_ns × 48000 / 10⁹) mod 2³²`, with `mediaclk:direct=0`.
- **SDP:**
  - Session name from the config (default `aas`).
  - `L24/48000/2`, `ptime:1`.
  - `ts-refclk` naming the grandmaster and domain from the clock layer.
  - `mediaclk:direct=0`.
  - Announced over SAP while sending.
- **No PTP time, no output.** While `now()` is `None` nothing is sent and the
  SAP announcement is withdrawn. On a new grandmaster the stream starts again
  and the SDP is announced with the new `ts-refclk`.
- **Silence is sent too.** A Dante subscription expects a continuous stream,
  so the output stops only when disabled, not when the mix is quiet.
- It is not a further output and is not counted in `align_frames`. Its delay
  never delays the master, as for AirPlay.

### Config

One `[aes67]` table. A device without it has no AES67.

```toml
[aes67]
interface = "eth0"
ptp = "listen"            # "listen", "off"; "ptp4l" in stage 2
domain = 0

[aes67.input]
enabled = true
stream = ""               # SDP session name; "" selects none
latency_ms = "auto"       # 1 to 100, or "auto": 3 on CM5/Pi 5, 20 otherwise
channels = [1, 2]
volume_db = 0

[aes67.output]
enabled = false
name = "aas"              # SDP session name
address = "auto"          # multicast group, or "auto"
port = 5004
ttl = 16
```

- The same rules as `[[airplay]]`: unknown keys are refused, and errors name
  the key.
- These can be changed at run time through the API:
  - input: `stream`, `latency_ms`, volume, mute and `enabled`;
  - output: `enabled`.
- Those changes are kept in the state file by the `Stored` rule: they apply
  while the config's value stays what it was.

### API

| Call | |
|---|---|
| `GET /aes67` | The clock: source, grandmaster, domain, locked, rate against local. Input: selected stream, mode (`ptp` or `buffer`), `receiving`, latency, late and lost packets. Output: enabled, sending, group, port. |
| `GET /aes67/streams` | Every announced stream: name, origin, group, port, format, channels, grandmaster, whether it can be selected and in which mode it would play. |
| `PATCH /aes67/input` | `stream` (a name, or `null`), `latency_ms` (a number or `"auto"`), `enabled`, `volume_db`, `mute`. |
| `PATCH /aes67/output` | `enabled`. |

- WebSocket `/events` sends `aes67` when any of it changes.
- The input's levels join the `/levels` frame. *(call)*

### ACR

The old agent posted `state_changed` to ACR as player `aes67`. `aas` takes
that over:

- `playing` while a stream is selected and receiving;
- `stopped` otherwise.

It posts to `http://127.0.0.1:1080/api/player/aes67/update` from a helper
thread, never the audio or control thread. A dead ACR costs a log line.

### Packaging in this repository

- `dist/aas.service`: `AmbientCapabilities=CAP_NET_RAW`,
  `CapabilityBoundingSet=CAP_NET_RAW`, with a comment saying why.
- README: an AES67 section covering:
  - the config;
  - the switch requirement (IGMP snooping with a querier);
  - wired only;
  - the Dante steps (AES67 mode on, a multicast flow for input, subscribing
    to the `aas` flow for output).

## Outside this repository, after stage 1

- **`hifiberry-aes67`:** dropped from the meta-packages. A transitional
  package removes it and its generated PipeWire drop-in.
- **Player descriptor:** `players.d/aes67.json` moves to the `aas`
  packaging in `hifiberry-os`, with `options_url` pointing at
  `/aes67/streams`.
- **The web UI page** (`hbos-ui`, `src/views/services/aes67.vue`) calls
  `aas`'s API.
- **`doc/switch.md`** stops listing AES67 as lost on the switch to `aas`. The
  PipeWire side loses AES67, as the owner chose.
- **Old settings:** the selection and latency the old agent stored are not
  carried over; a user selects the stream once more. *(call)*

## Left to the builder

- Threads beyond those named above, and whether SAP and the PTP listener
  share one.
- The listener's filter (window, outlier rule), and the cause of the
  unmatched Syncs in the test.
- The jitter buffer's layout and the `Drift` tuning for both directions. The
  controller's ±0.2 % limit is far above any crystal against PTP.
- How the input learns where a frame reaches the master, if the ring's fill
  and the configured output latency are not precise enough. Measure before
  adding anything.
- Tests:
  - SDP and SAP parsing from captured Dante announcements;
  - PTP parsing and the master comparison from captured packets;
  - the jitter buffer, and RTP timestamp arithmetic across wrap;
  - the clock layer against recorded packet streams;
  - the output sent to the input on one host with a fake clock.

  Capture the Dante packets (SAP, Announce, Sync, Follow_Up) from the test
  device early; they become the fixtures, with addresses and clock IDs
  replaced by placeholders.
